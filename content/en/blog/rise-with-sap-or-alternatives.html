Anyone looking into the end of maintenance for SAP ECC soon arrives at RISE with SAP. SAP presents it as the default route forward. Asking about RISE with SAP alternatives is still entirely reasonable, not because RISE is inherently a poor choice, but because a decision of this weight deserves a genuine comparison.

In projects I often see the offer already on the table before the organisation has written down its own requirements. The conversation then turns to price rather than to the right model. This article reverses that order: understand first, compare second, negotiate third.

What RISE with SAP actually is

RISE with SAP is not a single product but a bundle sold as a subscription. At its core sit SAP S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition, the infrastructure (at SAP or a hyperscaler), technical operations and transformation tooling. Depending on package and timing, further components were included, such as SAP Signavio, credits for SAP Business Technology Platform or SAP Build.

One thing to note for your own research: SAP renamed the package in 2025. The former product package is now called SAP Cloud ERP Private, while "RISE with SAP" has since come to describe the broader journey from on-premise ERP to the cloud. The new package is priced on the Full Use Equivalent (FUE) metric. The German-speaking user group DSAG was critical, seeing higher costs compared with the former RISE Premium. According to trade press reports, existing RISE customers can renew their contracts.

Nor is the division of work "all inclusive". SAP publishes a separate roles and responsibilities document that sets out which operational tasks SAP performs and which remain with the customer or require additional services.

One point deserves particular attention. In 2023 SAP announced that its newest innovations, including AI capabilities, would be delivered only through its cloud offerings. DSAG criticised this sharply at the time. If those capabilities matter to your business model, that is an argument for RISE; if they do not, the argument carries less weight.

Contract terms worth checking

A RISE contract runs for years. Clarify the following before you sign, ideally in writing and with worked examples. Please note that this is not legal advice; involve legal counsel when reviewing the contract.

  • Licence metric: how are FUEs calculated, how do user types count, and what happens if you grow or shrink?
  • Scope of service: which operational tasks does SAP actually perform, which stay with you, and what do extras cost?
  • Service levels: what availability is committed, and is it sufficient for your critical processes?
  • Term and renewal: how will prices develop on renewal, and are there caps?
  • Existing licences: how is your previous investment credited?
  • Exit: how, in what format and within what timeframe do you get your data back at the end of the contract?
  • Add-ons and AI: what is included, and what is licensed separately?

Many of these questions are closely tied to your current licence position. For more, see optimising SAP licences.

RISE with SAP alternatives at a glance

S/4HANA on-premise with perpetual licences

You buy licences, pay maintenance and run the system yourself or through a service provider, in your own data centre or on a hyperscaler. You keep more control over operations, release cycles and infrastructure. In return you carry more responsibility, and you should check which new functions are available on this route.

Staying on ECC with third-party support

A third-party provider takes over support for your existing system. That buys time but is no substitute for modernisation. I cover the options and deadlines in the article on ECC end of maintenance.

An ERP system from another vendor

The market is broader than the SAP debate often suggests. Established vendors include Microsoft Dynamics 365, Oracle and Infor. In the mid-market, proALPHA, abas and Odoo are among the systems in use. Each has its own focus, licensing model and partner ecosystem. I deliberately do not rank them: which system fits depends on your industry, size, international footprint and processes, and can only be settled through a structured selection.

Changing vendor always means a new implementation: processes, data migration, interfaces and training. It can make sense where SAP is oversized for your scale or industry, or where you intend to start afresh anyway.

You can recognise a good decision by the fact that you can still justify it in five years' time, whichever vendor won.

Next steps

  1. Write down your requirements and business objectives before requesting offers.
  2. Establish your current licences, maintenance contracts and actual usage.
  3. Shortlist two or three realistic options, including at least one alternative to RISE.
  4. Compare total cost over the same period for every option.
  5. Work through the contract terms above with each vendor.
  6. Decide using the weighted criteria, and document the reasoning.

Frequently asked questions

Is RISE with SAP the same as S/4HANA Cloud?

Not quite. RISE, now SAP Cloud ERP Private, is a commercial package built around S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition, with infrastructure and services. Alongside it there is the Public Edition, a more standardised SaaS offering.

Can I still run S/4HANA on-premise?

Yes, S/4HANA on-premise remains an option. Check which new functions SAP provides for this model, however, as SAP has said some innovations are reserved for its cloud offerings.

Is it worth switching to a different ERP vendor?

There is no general answer. A switch is worth considering when the functionality, cost or operating model of another system fits your requirements better, and you are prepared to take on the effort of a new implementation.
