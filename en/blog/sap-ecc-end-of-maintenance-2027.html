SAP ECC end of maintenance 2027: deadlines and options

Mainstream maintenance for SAP ECC 6.0 ends in 2027. This article sets out the deadlines, the realistic options and a way to reach a decision you can defend.

SAP ECCS/4HANAEnd of maintenanceERP strategy

The SAP ECC end of maintenance at the close of 2027 has been on the calendar for years, yet in many organisations it is only now becoming tangible. Roughly thirteen months remain. That is not much for an ERP change, and the dates beyond 2027 come with conditions you should understand before relying on them.

When I talk to IT directors and managing directors, I tend to hear one of two positions. Some assume they can "just carry on for a few more years". Others feel pushed into a decision they have not yet prepared properly. Both carry risk. This article lays out the facts and the options, without a vendor agenda.

The deadlines at a glance

SAP has communicated the following dates for SAP ERP 6.0 (ECC) and the other Business Suite 7 core applications:

PeriodWhat appliesConditions
Until end of 2025Mainstream maintenance for enhancement packages 0 to 5 has already endedIf you are still on an older EHP, you need to act now
Until end of 2027Mainstream maintenance for SAP ECC 6.0 / Business Suite 7Standard maintenance; also covers CRM 7.0, SCM 7.0 and SRM 7.0
2028–2030Extended maintenanceAn uplift of two percentage points on the maintenance basis
2031–2033"SAP ERP, private edition, transition option"Move to SAP ERP, private edition on SAP HANA by 31 December 2030, minimum system size of 2 TB, tied to the Max Success Plan; SAP states explicitly that it is not a maintenance extension for SAP ERP

SAP updated the transition option in October 2026: subscriptions may now run until 31 December 2035, subject to further prerequisites. Even so, it remains a time-limited bridge to the cloud, not a continuation of classic ECC.

For comparison: from the SAP S/4HANA 2023 release onwards, SAP gives each release seven years of mainstream maintenance and maintains its commitment to support S/4HANA until at least 2040.

The dates after 2027 are not a blanket extension. They cost more, depend on technical and contractual conditions, and only postpone the underlying question.

Your options at SAP ECC end of maintenance

There is no single right answer. The best route depends on your process landscape, the amount of custom code, the state of your data and where the business is heading.

1. Brownfield: system conversion

Your existing system is converted to S/4HANA. Processes, data and custom developments are largely retained. This is often the fastest route, but it also carries legacy baggage across. It makes sense when your core processes work well and you prefer to modernise step by step afterwards.

2. Greenfield: new implementation

You implement S/4HANA from scratch and align with the standard. This is an opportunity to clean up processes fundamentally, but it demands considerably more effort from the business, plus change management and training. Historical data is usually migrated only in part.

3. Bluefield or selective data transition

A middle path: you migrate selected data, organisational units or periods while redesigning other parts. It suits consolidations, carve-outs or groups where subsidiaries are at different stages. Methodologically it is more demanding and relies on specialist tooling.

4. RISE with SAP / SAP Cloud ERP Private

SAP bundles software, infrastructure and services into a subscription. This changes both the licensing and the operating model fundamentally. Whether it suits you deserves a deliberate assessment; see RISE with SAP or alternatives.

5. Third-party support

Providers such as Rimini Street offer support for SAP ECC 6.0 and advertise support through to 2040. That can buy time. Bear in mind, though, that you will not receive new releases or legal updates from SAP itself. Clarify up front on what terms a later return to SAP maintenance would be possible, and check any savings claims against your own contract.

6. Running on without maintenance

Technically, ECC will keep running after 2027. Without maintenance, however, you lose security patches, legal and regulatory changes (for tax or statutory reporting, for instance) and vendor support. For a productive core system that is a significant risk, defensible at most as a short and deliberately limited transition.

7. A different ERP

End of maintenance is also a natural moment to revisit the choice of system altogether. Besides SAP, there are established vendors such as Microsoft Dynamics 365, Oracle and Infor, and in the mid-market systems such as proALPHA, abas or Odoo. Switching is a full new implementation, with all the opportunity and effort that entails.

Decision criteria that actually matter

Rather than starting with the technology, I recommend structuring the decision around a few questions:

  • Process maturity: are today's processes a competitive advantage, or simply the result of history?
  • Custom code: how much custom code is in place, and how much of it is genuinely used?
  • Data quality: how clean are master data and open items? Poor data is expensive in every scenario.
  • People: will business experts and key users be available over the coming years?
  • Operating model: do you want to run the system yourself, use a service provider, or buy a subscription that includes operations?
  • Licences and contracts: what rights do you hold today, and what changes if you switch? More on this in optimising SAP licences.
  • Corporate strategy: are acquisitions, disposals or consolidations on the horizon that will reshape the landscape?

How much time you realistically need

Durations depend heavily on scope and starting point, so I deliberately avoid quoting generic figures. Qualitatively, a pure conversion is usually quicker than a new implementation, but it still needs a preparation phase, test cycles, several mock cutovers and a clean switchover. For what matters at go-live, see SAP cutover planning.

Three things are almost always underestimated: the availability of business experts, the clean-up of data and custom code, and the time management needs to make decisions. If you only start in 2027, you are effectively already planning with extended maintenance, and you should budget for it consciously.

Checklist: next steps

  1. Establish the current release and EHP level of your ECC systems.
  2. Review your maintenance contract: terms, notice periods and the cost of extended maintenance.
  3. Inventory custom developments and how much they are actually used.
  4. Assess master data quality through sampling.
  5. Define business objectives for the next five to ten years.
  6. Evaluate the options (brownfield, greenfield, selective, RISE, third-party support, a different ERP) against those objectives.
  7. Draw up a realistic roadmap with contingency and a budget for extended maintenance.
  8. Take the decision at board level, and record why.

Frequently asked questions

Will SAP ECC still work after 2027?

Yes, the system keeps running technically. Without valid maintenance, though, you receive no patches, no legal updates and no regular support. The risk grows each year.

Is the transition option an extension of ECC maintenance?

No. SAP is clear that it is not a maintenance extension for SAP ERP. It requires a move to SAP ERP, private edition on HANA, a minimum system size and the Max Success Plan.

Do I have to move to S/4HANA?

No. S/4HANA is the successor within the SAP portfolio, but third-party support or a different ERP system are legitimate options. What counts is what fits your strategy, not what a vendor prefers.
